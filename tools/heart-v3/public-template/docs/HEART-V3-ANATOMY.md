Internal blood cavities, walls, section faces, valve leaflets, annuli, AV chordae and papillary attachments are representative teaching reconstructions. They are not scanned chamber interiors. Chamber-specific deformation follows the numerical state, while geometric volume checks remain distinct from numerical conservation. Pale section faces are artificial teaching surfaces. The microscopic routes are authored enlargements without calibrated microscope magnification or patient-specific continuity.

The default Teaching exterior and its in-place Section use the same connected, moving authored myocardial envelope. Its boundary encloses the live teaching chamber surfaces. HRA reference remains a separate view: named regions and patient sidedness correspond, but scan-to-cavity geometric registration is not claimed. Per-chamber closed measurement proxies support volume checks; mathematical port caps are excluded from displayed anatomy. Neither the total envelope's tissue volume nor detailed tissue forces are calibrated.

Each valve has separate opening and attachment viewpoints. AV chordae terminate at actual moving leaflet vertices, with papillary bases attached to live wall vertices. Semilunar inspection sections remove overlying context to expose root cusps. Whole-body selection and hiding use an authored-region display classification, not HRA segmentation; isolated teaching chamber walls are explicitly identified as proxies.

AV chordae restrain leaflet prolapse; they are not opening motors. Semilunar cusps have no chordal apparatus. Blood flow, tissue magnification, electrical propagation and mechanism explanation are distinct routes. Filaments slide with changing overlap; the animation does not shorten actin or myosin filaments. Coronary red cells remain in vessels; exchange is a qualitative diffusion lesson, not an oxygen-transport solver.

## Four-chamber and blood-flow views

Four-chamber section reveals both atria and both ventricles within the same moving authored body, retaining both septa and the AV valves. The smaller atria are exposed by a dedicated section/camera; they are not relocated or inflated. Patient right is screen left in the preset; rotation changes screen sides. A separate Outflow view hides overlying atrial context to inspect RV→pulmonary valve→pulmonary trunk and LV→aortic valve→aorta. Return to Four-chamber section for the complete four-cavity overview.

Authored SVC/IVC terminals enter RA and pulmonary-vein terminals enter LA. These live teaching attachments have approximate HRA named-region correspondence, not measured source-lumen registration. Source surfaces, derivative geometry and rights remain unchanged. Selected RA/RV/LA/LV readouts and curves use that chamber’s own current model pressure, volume and activation; atrial receiving/contraction is distinct from ventricular isovolumetric phases.

Trace blood follows both circulations, including representative lung and systemic capillary insets that retain heart context. It is an itinerary across many beats, not a fifth microscopic route or a particle completing the circulation in one heartbeat. Coronary supply and additional cardiac-vein drainage remain Route D. Red/blue denotes oxygenation convention, not literal blood color or a quantitative oxygen solver. Pulmonary arteries are deoxygenated; pulmonary veins are oxygenated. Bulk blood stays in vessels while O₂ and CO₂ directions cross capillary barriers separately.

All directional cues use the current numerical sample. Valve passage is gated by actual forward Q/open state/pressure gradient. Integrated existing model Q determines qualitative marker displacement, not measured blood-cell transit. `valves.pulmonary.flow` is RV→pulmonary artery root inflow; `vascularFlows.pulmonary` is the separate pulmonary-bed flow. Likewise, aortic inflow differs from onward systemic flow: an arterial reservoir can supply its bed while its semilunar valve is closed. SVC/IVC and multiple venous branches illustrate aggregate edges, not measured branch flows or additional blood pools.

Nonvalve flows are signed. Atrial contraction can transiently raise model LA pressure above pulmonary venous-pool pressure and reverse `pulmonaryReturn`, while net cycle return remains toward LA. The current reverse arrow is preserved and identified as an uncalibrated lumped-model waveform. This is not mitral regurgitation; the separate normal valve edges remain one-way. Model accounting, the large ideal-valve peaks and these venous shapes are not clinical waveform validation.
